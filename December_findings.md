# Main findings so far

- Response-locked potential (RP) dissociates money vs. pain evidence accumulation
- Drift rate for money shows a positive relationship with RP amplitude
- Drift rate for pain shows a negative relationship with RP, but this effect is not significant

## Effects are localized around Cz

- ROI: 9 electrodes centered on Cz, consistent with Gluth et al. (2013) and Haggard-style RP work

## Boundary separation shows robust effects

- Boundary separation for both money and pain is negatively associated with RP amplitude

Effects appear in two response-locked windows:
- −0.4 to −0.2 s
- −0.2 to −0.1 s

Larger boundaries --> weaker RP

## Money-related parameters repeatedly dominate

Boundary separation for money predicts:
- RP strength (response-locked)
- N2 amplitude in cue-locked analysis

Suggests money signals influence both early control (boundary) and later motor/preparatory signals.

## Model specification matters & Results depend on regression setup

- Separate models (e.g. RP ~ v_money vs RP ~ v_pain, RT controlled)
- Joint models change relative contributions

# Measuring RP

## Vertex ROI

- 9 electrodes around parietal areas (Cp): (Jo et al.)
- The slope of each epoch was estimated by fitting a first order polynomial function to the averages of 9 electrodes around the vertex (FC1, FCz, FC2, C1, Cz, C2, CP1, CPz, and CP2) before the events
